The Living Past · Ocean Depth Zones · Goal #8

How loud should the depth ruler be?

The depth zones are the marine mirror of the soil strata and central to the accuracy thesis — but the underwater column is also the poster's most immersive void. Three treatments, from diagrammatic to nearly invisible. Each species still drops onto its true depth regardless; this only sets how visible the guide is.

Current

A · Diagrammatic

Bold dashed brass lines full-width + labels. Reads as a museum diagram. Maximum clarity, minimum immersion.
Recommended

B · Whisper from the edge

Thin brass hairlines that fade in only from the right edge and dissolve before crossing the immersive water; labels small at the margin. Accuracy stays legible; the void stays a void.
Alt

C · Labels only

No ruled lines at all — depth carried purely by the water gradient, with tiny zone labels tucked at the right margin. Most immersive, least explicit; risks the accuracy story reading as decoration.

Recommendation

B — whisper from the edge. The depth ruler is a selling point, so it can't vanish (rules out C's risk of looking like mood-lighting), but full-width dashed lines (A) turn the poster's most awe-inducing element into an infographic. Hairlines that fade in from the right margin and dissolve before they cross the open water keep the accuracy legible exactly where the eye checks a species' depth, while leaving the central void untouched.

This also mirrors the soil strata honestly: those are painted geological bands, not ruled lines — B gives the ocean the same painterly-but-labeled logic. Lock for §4: zone lines = thin brass hairlines, right-edge fade, small Optima labels at the margin.
