# The Living Past — Session Record, 2026-07-10

Plain-language log of everything we did today. (Locked decisions live in `SCOPE.md` and
`env_recipe.md`; this is the readable story.)

## What we set out to do
Two priorities the user set: (1) **design the page the QR code takes you to** before pouring
time into the T. rex, and (2) **level up the poster's plates** — they looked ~65% and needed
more flat land for the T. rex, plus a real dark abyss and accurate geology.

## What we accomplished

**1 · The QR landing page (organism page) — done.**
- Rebuilt `organism_page.html` to look like the poster: a **full-bleed cinematic hero** that
  plays the poster scene as an **animated video** (the user dropped an mp4 and it landed —
  autoplay, muted, loops, works on phones), the species name in flowing **Garamond italic**
  (fancy *and* taxonomically correct), all the "all about it" info below.
- Fixed legibility: the reading text is now **Optima** (clean), Garamond kept only for the big
  poetic lead line. Saved a snapshot at `organism_page.snapshot.html`.

**2 · The volume hub — done.** `volume_hub.html`: the cross-section as a "volume cover," then
all **32 species grouped by stratum** (Above Ground / Underground / Shoreline / Ocean), each a
card that links to its page. This is where the poster's master QR lands.

**3 · Phone scan test — worked.** Generated a QR pointing at the page over the local network
(`qr_scan_test.png`); the user scanned it and the animated page opened on their phone. The
whole "scan a poster → drop into the living scene" thesis landed for real.

**4 · The plates went to the next level.**
- **Composition study** (`plates/_composition_study.png`, `tools/compose_study.py`): balanced
  the scene around **3 focal points** — T. rex on a flat terrace, the volcano, the Mosasaurus
  from the abyss — with true-scale silhouettes proving the T. rex needs ~⅓ the poster width.
- **Black abyss (#6) — shipped.** `compose_proof.py` now depth-darkens the right ocean so
  sunlit stays bright but the deep falls to true black. In `_proof_scene.png`.
- **New base plate v2 — chosen.** The user generated many flat-terrace options; we picked a
  clean, "basic" one on purpose (a background should be a *stage*, not a performer). It gives
  the T. rex a real flat terrace, keeps the volcano, and already has a naturally dark deep.
  `plates/base_plate_v2.png`; `compose_proof.py` re-tuned to its geometry.
- **Accurate geology (#5) — built and composited.** The key insight: MJ can't do accurate
  strata, so **we own the structure, MJ owns the texture.**
  - `geology_hellcreek.json` — the real Hell Creek→Fox Hills→Pierre layer stack, referenced,
    with the honest detail that **no K–Pg boundary layer exists yet** (pre-impact).
  - `tools/render_strata.py` → `plates/_strata_accurate.png` — the accurate labeled column.
  - The user generated all **8 texture tiles**; `tools/composite_strata.py` clads the cliff by
    reprojecting each texture through the plate's own luminance and **warping the bands** so
    they dip naturally instead of looking like pasted stripes.
- **Result:** the poster cliff now reads as a real Hell Creek cross-section — rooted topsoil,
  coal seam, cross-bedded sandstone, the pale bentonite ash marker, down to the marine rocks —
  with the black abyss on the right and the flat terrace up top.

## Where we stand
- **QR destination: designed and proven on a phone.** Organism page + volume hub built.
- **The poster background: leveled up.** Flat T. rex terrace + black abyss + accurate
  stratified cliff, all from the user's new plates. This is the accuracy moat, visible.
- **Still the old base geometry's furniture** carries over cleanly; the volcano top is slightly
  cropped by the poster's scene band (easy fix next time).
