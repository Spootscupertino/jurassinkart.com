## Editorial frame

This dossier supports an original, family-readable account of an adult *Tyrannosaurus rex* in the terminal Maastrichtian Hell Creek–Lance coastal-plain world, about 68–66 million years ago. The public text contains no direct quotations and imports no source images, figures, maps, tables, or other third-party media.

Evidence labels used below:

- **Direct evidence:** an observation on a fossil or its geological context.
- **Supported inference:** an interpretation based on fossils, models, or comparison with living animals.
- **Artistic reconstruction:** a choice not determined by available evidence.

Confidence labels describe the claim as written, not the reputation of the source.

## Disputes, limitations, and language guardrails

### *Nanotyrannus* and growth

Zanno and Napoli (2025; R08) concluded that key small-bodied Hell Creek tyrannosaurs represent two species of *Nanotyrannus*, not juvenile *Tyrannosaurus*. Their paper explicitly calls for re-evaluation of hypotheses based on the older ontogenetic sequence. Consequently:

- Do not present the classic dramatic teenage growth curve as settled.
- Do not use “Jane,” the Cleveland skull, or the Dueling Dinosaurs tyrannosaur as an unquestioned juvenile *T. rex*.
- Bone histology still demonstrates that dinosaurs recorded growth in their tissues, but the specimen membership of a *T. rex*-only growth sample must be re-audited.
- The content payload describes the 2025 result and the need for re-testing; it does not claim that every researcher now agrees or supply a replacement growth rate.

### Hunter versus scavenger

The healed hadrosaur injury (R04) establishes an attack on living prey, not the proportion of calories obtained by hunting. Bite traces and bone consumption do not distinguish every hunted carcass from every scavenged one. Use “could hunt and likely also scavenged,” not “only hunter,” “only scavenger,” or a quantified diet split.

### Speed and locomotion

No top speed is given. Estimates vary with mass, muscle reconstruction, posture, safety factors, and modeling method. The balanced bipedal stance is secure; a sprint speed is not necessary for this page.

### Skin, feathers, lips, and color

R06 directly samples scales only from limited body regions. “Predominantly scaly adult” is a defensible synthesis; “entirely featherless” overstates the fossils. No color pattern is known. The appearance of the oral margin in life remains debated and is intentionally not adjudicated here.

### Vision, smell, hearing, and voice

Binocular overlap is modeled from reconstructed head geometry (R05), not observed behavior. Strong smell/hearing are comparative neuroanatomical inferences. No fossil establishes a lion-like roar. The poster’s open mouth is a storytelling moment, not evidence of a particular vocalization.

### Body size

Length can be estimated from skeletons; mass requires restoring missing soft tissue. R02 illustrates the sensitivity of volumetric estimates. The public text therefore uses “many tonnes,” not one supposedly exact mass.

### Geographic scope

The page is deliberately anchored in the Hell Creek and Lance formations. Claims about far-southern occurrences or the exact continental range are excluded because fragmentary material and changing taxonomy make the margins less secure.

## Deeper Dive selection rationale

Exactly three public paths appear in the payload:

1. **Current research:** Zanno & Napoli (2025), because it demonstrates science actively revising a famous animal.
2. **Historic specimen:** AMNH’s *T. rex* page, because it connects a real fossil to the changing reconstruction of posture.
3. **Whole ecosystem:** Smithsonian Deep Time virtual tour, because it places the Nation’s T. rex within environments, evolution, and extinction.

These are editorial recommendations only. None of the named authors or institutions has participated in, reviewed, or endorsed The Living Past.

## Verification gaps

- I did not verify a species-wide maximum body mass precise enough to publish as a single number; the payload intentionally uses “many tonnes.”
- I did not find direct evidence for a specific roar, color pattern, pack behavior, running speed, or routine hunting method; none is asserted.
- The exact southern and northern limits of the species’ geographic range were not established strongly enough for a precise map boundary.
- The 2025 *Nanotyrannus* result changes the membership of older growth datasets; a replacement, consensus *T. rex*-only growth curve is not yet available in the consulted source set.
- The exact extent of feathers or filaments on unsampled body regions cannot be determined from the known skin impressions.
